Why did I write The Quiet Frequency?

Because I was tired of alien stories that start with explosions. I wanted one that starts with a whisper.

Slade Quintz is a retired spy hiding out at Cape Sorrow, a decommissioned weather station on the foggy Oregon coast. His roommates are Cipher, an aging Malinois with a bad hip, and Sebastion, a duct-taped hotel-concierge robot who steeps tea he can't drink. Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

It's a voice. A dying colony of living light, high above Earth, blacking out the grid whenever it calls for help. (Sebastion named it Lumen, since calling it 'it' seemed rude.)

Underneath the thriller, it's really a story about guilt, second chances, and how the hardest part of saving anything can be staying. I wanted dry humor sitting right on top of real heartbreak.

The Quiet Frequency is a science fiction thriller for readers of character-driven, wry first-contact sci-fi.
