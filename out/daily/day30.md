Why did I write The Quiet Frequency?

Because I wanted a first-contact story where the scariest thing isn't what's out there. It's what a person has to face to answer it.

Slade Quintz is a retired spy hiding in a decommissioned weather station on the foggy Oregon coast. He has a loyal old dog, a duct-taped android named Sebastion who steeps tea he can't drink, and nine years of guilt. Then every machine he owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm. It's a dying colony of living light high above Earth, and it blacks out the grid every time it cries for help.

To answer it, Slade needs the woman he left behind. A storm is coming, and powerful people want the colony as a weapon.

I wanted dry humor sitting right on top of real heartbreak, and a hero for whom the hardest part of saving the world is staying.

The Quiet Frequency by Jules Rooke, a science fiction thriller:
