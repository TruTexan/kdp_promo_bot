Title: What's the best science fiction thriller you read this year? I'm especially curious about quieter, character-driven ones

Body:
I've been on a first-contact and slow-burn sci-fi thriller kick, and I'm trying to work out what makes the good ones stick.

For me it's rarely the size of the threat. It's when the story stays small and personal while the stakes get huge. A few things that work on me every time:

- A protagonist with real baggage, not just a tragic backstory pinned on for flavor
- Humor that shows up *because* things are bleak, not in spite of it
- A setting that feels like a character (isolated, weather-beaten, slightly abandoned)
- Tech or alien contact that's strange and mysterious instead of explained away in chapter three
- A thriller engine that kicks in late, after you already care about the people

So, two questions:

1. What's the best sci-fi thriller you read this year, new or old?
2. What was the one thing it did that made you stay up too late?

Bonus round: what's the best sidekick you've come across, human, animal, or machine? I have a soft spot for deadpan robots and dogs who refuse to admit they're in pain.

Full disclosure, since it's relevant: I'm an author, and I recently published a sci-fi thriller in this vein (a burned-out retired spy, a duct-taped android butler, and a dying alien intelligence knocking out the power grid on the Oregon coast). I'm not here to pitch it. I'm genuinely after reading recommendations, and I'm happy to share the title if anyone asks or if the mods say it's okay.

Looking forward to adding some of your picks to my list. Tell me what I should read next.
