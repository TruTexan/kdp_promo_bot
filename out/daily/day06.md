Title: First contact stories are usually about talking. I wanted to write one about listening, and about what it costs to stay.

Body:

Disclosure up front: I'm the author of a book that grew out of this idea, but I'm posting because I'd like to hear what other people think, not to sell anything.

Most first contact fiction I love hinges on a translation problem. How do we decode them? What do they want? But the versions that stuck with me are really about attention. Whether anyone is willing to sit with something strange and unfinished long enough to understand it, without rushing to weaponize it, fear it, or fix it.

I've been chewing on a few threads that I think make first contact work emotionally:

1. **Listening is an act of trust.** To really listen you have to stop controlling the conversation. That's uncomfortable for institutions, and for people who survived by never staying open.

2. **Being heard is the real stakes.** The signal in a good first contact story is rarely just information. It's a request. Someone out there is saying "is anyone there?" and the plot turns on who answers, and why.

3. **Betrayal hits harder when the stakes are intimate.** Governments grabbing alien tech is expected. What lands is a person letting down another person at the worst possible moment, and whether they get a second chance to show up.

4. **Found family beats lone-genius heroics.** Give me a burned-out ex-operative, an old dog with a bad hip, and a duct-taped robot who steeps tea he can't drink over a chosen one any day.

5. **The hardest heroic act is often staying.** Not the explosion or the sprint, but remaining in the room, the relationship, the fight, when every instinct says run.

Those threads are what I built my novel around. The premise: a retired spy hiding at a dead weather station on the foggy Oregon coast suddenly finds every machine he owns pulsing to the same rhythm. It turns out to be a voice, a dying colony of living light high above Earth that blacks out the grid whenever it cries for help. To answer it he needs the physicist he walked out on nine years earlier, and a storm is coming along with people who'd like to turn the colony into a weapon. I went for first-person present tense, dry humor over real heartbreak, and quiet atmosphere that tightens into a thriller.

What I'm curious about:

- Which first contact stories made you feel the "listening" part most? (Contact, Story of Your Life, Solaris, and Project Hail Mary come to mind for me, each in a very different way.)
- Do you prefer the alien to stay mysterious, or do you want it to become a character you can love?
- Are there stories where the human relationships mattered more to you than the big sci-fi idea?
- Any burned-out-spy-meets-the-impossible books I should be reading?
