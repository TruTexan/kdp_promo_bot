# The End of the World Sounds Like a Whisper

## A Signal Worth Listening To

Retired spy Slade Quintz has spent years hiding at Cape Sorrow, a decommissioned weather station at the end of four miles of cracked road on the fog-bound Oregon coast. His company is Cipher, an eight-year-old Malinois with a bad hip he'll never admit to, and Sebastion, a duct-taped concierge android who steeps tea he can't drink.

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

## First Contact, Told Quietly

It's a voice: a dying colony of living light high above Earth, blacking out the grid every time it cries for help. Sebastion names it Lumen, because calling it "it" seems rude.

*The Quiet Frequency* is a science fiction thriller told in first-person, present tense, with dry humor over real heartbreak. It starts out quiet and atmospheric, then turns into a thriller once the feds arrive.

## For Fans of Project Hail Mary and Slow Horses

If you like wry, character-driven first-contact stories where understanding a stranger matters as much as the science, you may feel at home here. The same goes if you like burned-out spies who carry their guilt like luggage. The book explores:

- Listening and being heard
- Trust and betrayal
- Second chances
- Found family
- The courage to stay

## The Hardest Part Is Staying

To answer Lumen, Slade needs Dr. Marisol Vega, the physicist whose paper on living plasma in the sky was dismissed as "a fairy tale in LaTeX." She is also the woman he abandoned nine years ago.

A storm is coming, and powerful people want the colony as a weapon. Saving Lumen will cost everything. For Slade, the hardest part isn't running toward danger. It's staying.

## Start Listening

*The Quiet Frequency* by Jules Rooke: a dying alien mind, a burned-out spy, one last signal.
