THE QUIET FREQUENCY by Jules Rooke
"The end of the world sounds like a whisper."

A dying alien mind. A burned-out spy. One last signal.

Slade Quintz has spent years hiding at Cape Sorrow, a decommissioned weather station at the end of four miles of cracked road on the fog-bound Oregon coast. His company is a Malinois named Cipher, who has a bad hip and will never admit it, and a duct-taped android named Sebastion. Sebastion was rescued from a scrapyard for $20 and a six-pack, and he steeps tea he can't drink.

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

It's a voice. A dying colony of living light, high above Earth, is blacking out the grid every time it cries for help. Sebastion named it Lumen, because calling it "it" seemed rude.

To answer it, Slade needs the one person who might understand: Dr. Marisol Vega, the physicist whose theory was dismissed as "a fairy tale in LaTeX." She's also the woman he abandoned nine years ago. A storm is coming, and powerful people want Lumen as a weapon.

For Slade, the hardest part is staying.

---

WHY IT MIGHT BE YOUR KIND OF BOOK

• First-person, present tense, with dry humor over real heartbreak
• Sebastion's deadpan butler wit against Slade's guilt
• Quiet, foggy, atmospheric first contact that turns into a thriller once the feds arrive
• One week of fog and storm, from a lonely coastal station to a sealed Cold War bunker
