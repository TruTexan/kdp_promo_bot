# Why Early Readers Are Finishing The Quiet Frequency in One Sitting

A few early readers have told us the same thing: they planned to read a chapter or two, then looked up and the book was done. We can't promise your evening will go the same way, but here is what seems to keep the pages turning.

## A Whisper That Won't Let Go

Retired spy Slade Quintz has spent years hiding at Cape Sorrow, a dead weather station on the fog-bound Oregon coast. His company is a loyal Malinois named Cipher and a duct-taped android named Sebastion. Then every machine he owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

It's a voice: a dying colony of living light high above Earth, blacking out the grid every time it cries for help.

## A Week With No Room to Breathe

The story unfolds over one week of fog and storm. That tight window keeps the pressure building. Slade needs Dr. Marisol Vega, the physicist whose theory was dismissed as "a fairy tale in LaTeX" and the woman he abandoned nine years ago. Meanwhile, Director Amelia Hartwell, with her clean shoes and her own plans for the colony, is closing in.

## Dry Humor, Real Heartbreak

Slade narrates in first person, present tense, with wit that never quite covers the guilt underneath. Sebastion's deadpan butler commentary (he steeps tea he can't drink) balances the ache. Cipher, for his part, will never admit his hip hurts.

## Quiet First, Then Full Thriller

The opening chapters are atmospheric and intimate, with static in the walls and a signal nobody can explain. Once the feds arrive, the book shifts gears into a thriller, and that change in pace may be why readers keep going.

## Who This Book Is For

If you like character-driven, wry first-contact science fiction or burned-out-spy thrillers, this one was written for you.

*The end of the world sounds like a whisper.*

## Start Listening

*The Quiet Frequency* by Jules Rooke is available now on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HS8PVDY6
