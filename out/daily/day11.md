The end of the world sounds like a whisper.

That's the idea behind *The Quiet Frequency*, a science fiction thriller by Jules Rooke: a dying alien mind, a burned-out spy, and one last signal.

## A Signal Over the Oregon Coast

Retired spy Slade Quintz has spent years hiding at Cape Sorrow, a decommissioned weather station at the end of four miles of cracked road above Port Halloran. His company is an eight-year-old Malinois named Cipher, who has a bad hip and will never admit it hurts, and a duct-taped android named Sebastion, who steeps tea he can't drink.

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

## A Voice Made of Light

It's a voice. A dying colony of living light, high above Earth, is blacking out the grid every time it cries for help. Sebastion has named it Lumen, because calling it "it" seemed rude.

## The Woman He Left Behind

To answer Lumen, Slade needs Dr. Marisol Vega, the physicist whose paper on living plasma in the sky was dismissed as "a fairy tale in LaTeX." She is also the woman he abandoned nine years ago.

A storm is coming. Director Amelia Hartwell, with her clean shoes and a plan, wants Lumen as a weapon. Saving the colony will cost Slade everything, and for a man who has spent years running, the hardest part is staying.

## What the Story Feels Like

The book is told in first-person, present tense, with dry humor over real heartbreak. Sebastion's deadpan butler wit balances Slade's guilt. The story begins quiet and atmospheric, set across one week of fog and storm, then becomes a full thriller once the feds arrive.

## Who It's For

If you like character-driven, wry first-contact sci-fi and burned-out-spy thrillers, Cape Sorrow might be your kind of hideout.

## Start Listening

The signal is already broadcasting. [Find *The Quiet Frequency* on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HS8PVDY6).
