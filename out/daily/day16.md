Some stories are loud. This one starts with a whisper.

In The Quiet Frequency, retired spy Slade Quintz has spent years hiding at Cape Sorrow, a decommissioned weather station on the foggy Oregon coast. His company: an aging Malinois named Cipher, and Sebastion, a duct-taped concierge android who steeps tea he can't drink.

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

It's a voice. A dying colony of living light high above Earth, blacking out the grid every time it cries for help. (Sebastion named it Lumen, because calling it "it" seemed rude.)

To answer it, Slade needs the woman he abandoned nine years ago. A storm is coming, and powerful people want Lumen as a weapon.

Underneath the first-contact thriller, it's a story about being heard, trust and betrayal, second chances, found family, and the courage to stay when leaving is so much easier.
