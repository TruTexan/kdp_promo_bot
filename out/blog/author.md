# Why I Wrote *The Quiet Frequency*

## The Question I Can't Stop Asking

I keep returning to one question: what does a person do when the thing he's been hiding from finally knocks? Not kicks the door in, just knocks, politely, in a rhythm only he can hear.

That's where *The Quiet Frequency* starts. Slade Quintz is a retired spy who has spent years at Cape Sorrow, a dead weather station on the Oregon coast. He has a dog named Cipher, a view of a very large ocean, and a plan to feel nothing in either direction. It's a good plan. I know because I wrote it for him, and then I spent a whole book dismantling it.

## A Butler Held Together with Duct Tape

Slade does not live entirely alone. He lives with Sebastion, an android who is, at best, structurally optimistic. Duct tape is involved. So is a level of deadpan that I can only describe as professional.

I didn't plan Sebastion as comic relief. I needed someone who could say the quiet part out loud, and it turns out a butler is the perfect delivery system. He can observe that his employer is wallowing, and do it so politely that the insult arrives a full minute later, like thunder.

Slade is carrying real guilt, including a woman he abandoned nine years ago. Without Sebastion, the book would sink under it. With him, it floats just enough to keep breathing.

## Why the Signal Is Light

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

I wanted the threat to be strange and a little beautiful, not a ticking bomb or a villain's monologue. The pulse turns out to be a voice: a dying colony of living light, high above Earth, blacking out the grid every time it cries for help. It isn't attacking anyone. It's asking.

That mattered to me. Most of us know what it's like to be the one who hears the cry and pretends the phone isn't ringing. I wanted to write about what happens when the call is too loud to ignore, and answering it means going back to the people you left.

## And Then the Feds Arrive

I'll be honest about the shape of the book. The first stretch is quiet and atmospheric: fog, static, a man and his dog and his tired robot, listening. I wanted readers to sit in that stillness long enough to feel how fragile it is.

Then the storm comes, and so do the people who see the colony as a weapon. The pace changes. The quiet doesn't vanish so much as get hunted. Slade's old skills wake up, and so does everything he's been avoiding.
