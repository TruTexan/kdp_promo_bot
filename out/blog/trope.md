# The Quiet Frequency: Notes from Cape Sorrow

## The Setup

I begin with a man who wants nothing. Slade Quintz is a retired spy who has chosen a dead weather station on the Oregon coast for exactly that reason. Cape Sorrow has fog, gulls, and a ruined view. It has no one asking him for anything.

He has a dog named Cipher, who is a better listener than most people I know. He also has an android named Sebastion, held together with duct tape and a stubborn sense of decorum. Sebastion serves tea like the apocalypse is a scheduling conflict.

Then every machine Slade owns starts pulsing to the same rhythm.

## The Voice

This is where the book turns, and I'm not going to pretend I saw it coming. I thought I was writing about a man in hiding. It turns out I was writing about a signal.

It isn't a glitch. It's a voice, and it comes from a dying colony of living light high above Earth. Each time it cries for help, the grid goes dark. It's a terrible way to ask for something, and it's also the only way it has.

I keep returning to that. Being heard is hard enough when you speak the same language. It's harder when your only tool is a blackout.

## Humor, Because Otherwise I'd Have to Feel Things Directly

Slade carries a lot of guilt, and I wanted it handled honestly, with no speeches about it. That's Sebastion's job. He delivers his dry observations with a straight face, and they land exactly where Slade's defenses are thinnest. A line that would be a joke in another book is, in this one, a small act of love.

I like writing these two together. Slade can't forgive himself, and Sebastion won't make a fuss about it. Somewhere between those positions is the closest thing Cape Sorrow has to a family. It's a found one, assembled from a man, a dog, and a machine that insists on proper tea service.

## The Part Where It Gets Loud

The early chapters are quiet and atmospheric. Static, salt air, an old station humming in the dark. I wanted the reader to sit in that stillness long enough to feel what Slade stands to lose.

But the book doesn't stay quiet. Once the feds arrive, it becomes a thriller. Powerful people want the colony as a weapon, a storm is coming, and answering the signal will require something Slade has avoided for nine years: the woman he abandoned. He needs her. She has every reason to say no.

## What I Was Really Writing About

On the surface, it's first contact. Underneath, I was writing about trust and betrayal, about second chances you don't feel you've earned, and about what it means to be answered after a long silence.

Mostly I was writing about staying. Running is easy for Slade, since he's had decades of practice. Staying in the room, in the storm, in the consequences, is the hard part.
